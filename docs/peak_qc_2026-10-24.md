# Are the H3K27me3 peaks the right size?

2026-10-24. Checked against the gates in the project's own QC doc:
over-called >35% genome coverage (broad marks), under-called <0.5%, and the
~5% that a 2-of-3 epic2 consensus typically covers for a broad mark.

**Short answer: both, badly, depending on which set.** Nothing here is
currently usable as a thresholding substrate without being re-called.

## ENCODE human — far too small

The peaks behind `EncodeHuman_me3_peaktss_aggregated.txt`. Format is MACS2
narrowPeak (10 columns with a summit offset), i.e. a **sharp caller used on a
broad mark**, where the QC doc specifies epic2.

| sample | n peaks | median bp | mean bp | total Mb | genome % | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GM12878 | 21,365 | 325 | 390 | 8.3 | **0.27** | under-called |
| H1 | 25,835 | 645 | 1,298 | 33.5 | 1.08 | low |
| HepG2 | 135,407 | 424 | 539 | 73.0 | 2.35 | low |
| K562 | 74,264 | 268 | 307 | 22.8 | 0.73 | low |
| liver | 7,623 | 401 | 468 | 3.6 | **0.11** | under-called |
| SK-N-SH | 94,828 | 239 | 336 | 31.8 | 1.03 | low |

Median widths of 239–645 bp for a mark that forms kb-scale domains. Coverage
is 2–45× below the ~5% expectation, and GM12878 and liver fail the <0.5%
under-called gate outright.

Only **3.0–25.5%** of TSS windows overlap any called peak:

| | GM12878 | H1 | HepG2 | K562 | liver | SK-N-SH |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TSS windows with a peak | 5.5% | 14.8% | 25.5% | 3.7% | 3.0% | 11.2% |

**This corrects something I said earlier.** I read the 1.9–10.7% of exact
zeros in the aggregated table as "genes with no overlapping peak". It is not —
genes without a peak still get counts from the plain TSS window, so the table
is a *mixture* of peak-extended and fallback quantifications, and the zeros
are just genes with no reads.

## West mouse cerebellum — appropriately broad, but the quant measures width

Window widths average 30.8 kb (max 469 kb), which is the right scale for
H3K27me3. The problem is what the column contains. Over 19,237 genes in
P14 rep1:

- `corr(log window width, log count + 1)` = **0.957**
- median count/kb is flat across every width bin: 8.0, 7.8, 7.3, 8.3

So the raw count is essentially window width times a near-constant density.
**Thresholding it would be thresholding domain size, not signal intensity.**
It needs dividing by window width, or replacing with a fold-over-input
density, before any mixture or null is applied. 11.6% of genes fall back to
the 500 bp window, so the widths vary by ~60× across rows.

Re-running at `gap=1` does not rescue them — the same six stay at 64–82%
(P12 r1 80.0, P12 r2 74.6, P12 r4 81.8, P21 r3 80.4, P7 r1 64.0, P7 r2 32.0),
while EL_GNP1/Tumor1/Tumor4 stay sane at 4.5/3.9/6.3%. **So this is library
quality, not a tuning choice**, consistent with the QC doc's note that the
West RSC exposes poor enrichment.

## What follows

1. The ENCODE human me3 peaks need re-calling with epic2, not MACS2, before
   the peak∩TSS tables mean anything.
2. The West peaktss counts need width normalisation whatever happens to the
   peak calls.
3. The six over-called West replicates (P7 r1/r2, P12 r1/r2/r4, P21 r3) and
   the three under-called ones (P12 r3, P21 r2/r5) should be dropped or
   re-sequenced rather than re-tuned.
4. GBM, the 20 ENCODE mouse tissues and GNP have no me3 peak sets in this
   pipeline at all, so the lattice dataset remains on raw TSS windows.
